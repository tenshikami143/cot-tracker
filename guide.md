A plain-language guide for a complete beginner. It is for learning, not financial advice. The example numbers come from the report for positions as of Nov 17, 2026, so the numbers you see later will be different.

## 1. The big idea in one minute

Every week, the US government (an agency called the CFTC) publishes a report called the Commitments of Traders, or COT. It shows how big traders are betting in the futures market. Futures are contracts to buy or sell something at a set price later, and there are futures for currencies, gold, oil, stock indexes and more.

Think of it as a weekly peek at what the big players are doing with their money. Most traders only look at price charts. COT adds a second question: who is betting on which side?

Your coach was right. Many traders get so busy with signals and tools that they forget two basic things: what the big players are doing, and the news. COT covers the first one. Section 6 covers the news.

## 2. When does the report come out, and how old is it?

- It measures positions as of **Tuesday**.
- It is published on **Friday** afternoon (US Eastern time). Around US holidays it can come out a day or more late.
- So by the time you read it, the data is already about 3 days old, and it stays the same until the next Friday.

That is why COT is a background guide and not a timing tool. It tells you the mood of the big players over weeks, not what to do in the next hour.

## 3. The three groups

The report splits traders into three groups. The tracker has a button for each one.

| Group | Who they are | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Large Speculators | Hedge funds and big traders betting to make a profit | A fund that thinks the euro will fall and sells euro futures |
| Commercials | Businesses that use futures to protect themselves from price swings (hedgers) | A company that sells goods in euros and locks in a rate |
| Small Traders | Smaller accounts below the reporting level | Individual traders like you |

Most forex traders watch the **Large Speculators** first, because they are betting on direction. Commercials are hedging, so they often sit on the opposite side. When Large Speculators and Commercials are at opposite extremes, some traders see that as a sign the move is stretched. It is context, not a signal on its own.

## 4. How to read the numbers

Let us use a real row. Euro, Large Speculators, positions as of Nov 17, 2026:

| Long | Short | Net | Weekly change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 230,074 | 372,784 | -142,710 | -6,314 |

- **Long** means contracts betting the euro will go **up**.
- **Short** means contracts betting the euro will go **down**.
- **Net** is Long minus Short: 230,074 - 372,784 = -142,710. A negative net means more bets on down than on up. The big traders are net **bearish** on the euro.
- **Weekly change** is how much the net moved since last week. Here it went from -136,396 to -142,710, so they became a bit **more** bearish.

In the tracker, green numbers mean up and red numbers mean down.

### The COT Index (0 to 100)

A net of -142,710 sounds huge, but is it big compared with normal? The COT Index answers that. It looks back over the past 52 weeks and finds the most bearish net and the most bullish net. Then it shows where today's net sits between them.

- **0** means the most bearish it has been in the past year.
- **100** means the most bullish it has been in the past year.
- **50** means right in the middle.

It is like rating how hungry you are compared with your own past year. A 9 out of 10 is a big deal for you, even if it is normal for someone else. That is why the index lets you compare markets fairly, even when their raw numbers are very different in size.

The euro's index is 4 this week, so the big traders are almost as bearish on it as they have been all year.

### Why a scale of 0 to 100?

The raw net number is hard to judge on its own, so the index turns it into a score between 0 and 100. It takes the lowest and highest net of the past 52 weeks and shows where today's net sits between them.

Example with made-up numbers: the past year ranged from -150,000 (lowest) to +50,000 (highest), and today's net is -100,000. Today is 50,000 above the lowest point, out of a 200,000 range. That is 25%, so the index is **25**.

The same method works for the euro, gold, oil or Bitcoin, even though their raw numbers are very different in size. One catch: 0 means the lowest in the window, not that it cannot go lower. If the net keeps falling, the index stays at 0.

### Net sign versus index

These answer different questions, so check both.

- The **net sign** (plus or minus) tells you which side the big traders are on.
- The **index** tells you how strong that is compared with the past year.

Example: the Swiss franc has an index of 100 but a net of -8,281 (slightly negative). The traders are still net short, but they are less bearish than at any time this year. The label says Bearish because the net is negative, while the index says the mood has swung as far toward bullish as it has all year. Neither is wrong. They are two different facts.

### Reading the index together with the net

Below 50 means the big traders are more bearish than usual for the past year. Above 50 means more bullish than usual. This describes their mood compared with their own normal, so always check the net sign too:

| Index | Net | Plain reading |
|---|---|---|
| Low (under 50) | Negative | Bearish, and more bearish than usual |
| Low (under 50) | Positive | Still bullish, but fading (example: MXN, index 15, net +25,247) |
| High (over 50) | Positive | Bullish, and more bullish than usual |
| High (over 50) | Negative | Still bearish, but improving (example: GBP, index 80, net -32,304) |

The first and third rows are the clean cases, where the index and the net agree. In the second and fourth rows the mood is changing, so be more careful.

## 5. Extremes and crowded trades

When the index is 10 or below, or 90 or above, the tracker calls it an **Extreme**.

Picture a small boat where everyone has rushed to one side. It is leaning a lot, and there is nobody left to push it further. Now even a small wave can tip it back the other way. In markets, if nearly every big trader has already bet against a currency, there are few sellers left. A small piece of good news can make them rush to close their bets, and the price jumps the other way. That is why extremes come with a warning to watch for a reversal.

Crowding can happen at either end of the scale. An index near 0 means a crowd on the short side, so a sharp bounce is the risk. An index near 100 means a crowd on the long side, so a sharp drop is the risk. Around 50 means no crowd either way. A 95 is just as crowded as a 5, only on the opposite side.

But remember:

- Extremes can last for **weeks or months**. An index of 0 is a warning sign, not a countdown.
- The boat can keep leaning. Do not trade against a strong trend only because an extreme says so.

### Common beginner mistakes

1. Treating an extreme as an instant signal to buy or sell. It is not a timing tool.
2. Forgetting that the data is from Tuesday.
3. Using COT alone. Combine it with the news and your chart.
4. Mixing up the net sign and the index.
5. Forgetting that COT is futures data. It is close to, but not the same as, the spot forex price you trade on your platform.

### How the COT Index helps you decide

The index does not tell you when to buy or sell. It tells you what kind of trade is worth looking for, and when to be careful.

1. **Choosing a side.** If the big traders are heavily bearish on the euro (index 4) and bullish on the dollar (76), selling EUR/USD has the big money behind it. Look for sell setups on your chart first.
2. **Picking the stronger and weaker currency.** The best candidates are a currency near the top of its range against one near the bottom. GBP/USD (80 against 76) has no clear gap.
3. **Spotting crowded trades.** At an extreme, use a smaller position and a closer stop-loss, and do not chase a price that has already moved a lot.
4. **Skipping conflicts.** If your chart says buy but the big traders are heavily short, you do not have to trade it.

Treat COT as one vote out of three, along with the news and your chart. When two or three agree, the setup is stronger. When they disagree, it is fine to wait. The index cannot give the exact moment to enter, the big traders can be wrong, and the data is about three days old when it is published.

## 6. Why the news matters too

Your coach said traders lose trades because they skip the news and COT. Here is the news side in plain words.

Scheduled events can move prices fast, even against a good setup. Examples:

- **Interest rate decisions** by central banks (the Fed for the US dollar, the ECB for the euro, the RBA for the Australian dollar).
- **Inflation reports (CPI)** and **jobs reports** (like the US Non-Farm Payrolls).

Example: your COT lean says sell EUR/USD, and your chart looks fine. But a big US jobs report comes out in two hours. The price could jump many pips in a minute. You want to know that before you open the trade, so you can wait, use a smaller size, or stay out.

A simple habit: check an economic calendar every morning. You already have the Forex Factory calendar bookmarked. Look for the red (high impact) events for the currencies you trade.

## 7. Using the tracker, step by step

This page lives at **gm-cot-tracker.streamlit.app**. Bookmark it. You can reopen this guide any time from the Beginner's guide box at the top.

### The controls at the top

- **Who are we looking at?** Pick Large Speculators, Commercials or Small Traders. Start with Large Speculators.
- **Index looks back (weeks):** leave this at **52**. It goes back to 52 every time you reload the page.
- **Sort by:** puts the table in order, for example by lowest or highest COT Index, or by the biggest weekly change.
- **Extremes only:** hides everything except the markets at an extreme.
- **Refresh:** fetches the newest numbers. The page keeps its data for about 6 hours before downloading again.

### My pairs

This is the section near the top with four cards, one for each of your pairs: EUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD and XAU/USD (gold). Each card has:

1. **A lean.** LEAN BUY, LEAN SELL or NO CLEAR LEAN. It compares the two currencies. If the first currency sits much higher in its range than the second, the lean is buy. Much lower means sell.
2. **A strength.** Strong, moderate or weak.
   - It needs a gap of at least 30 index points to show any lean at all.
   - It is **strong** only when the gap is 60 or more **and** the net positions of both currencies agree with it.
   - It is **weak** when both net positions go against the lean.
3. **An index line**, for example: EUR index 4 (net short) vs USD index 76 (net long).
4. **Notes.** These warn about crowded trades, or say something like 'GOLD is still net long, so the sell lean is only partly supported'.
5. **A Commercials line.** It says whether the Commercials lean the same way, the other way, or have no clear lean. Leaning the other way is common, since they hedge. It matters most when both groups are stretched on opposite sides.
6. **A menu called 'chart trend today'.** This is the daily part.

#### The daily part

COT changes only once a week, so the lean looks the same from Monday to Friday. To add a daily check, open your MT4 daily chart, look at which way the price has been moving, and pick **Up**, **Down** or **Sideways** in the menu. The card then tells you:

| What the card says | What it means |
|---|---|
| Aligned | COT and your chart agree. That is the best case, but it still needs a news check. |
| Conflict | COT leans one way and your chart goes the other. Wait for more confirmation. |
| Chart is sideways | The COT lean has no trend support yet. |
| No clear lean | COT is not helping here. Rely on your other tools. |

Your menu choices reset when you reload the page, so set them each day.

Example: the card says LEAN SELL on EUR/USD. Your chart shows the price falling and sitting below its moving averages, so you pick Down. The card says Aligned. That is two tools agreeing. Now check the news calendar before you decide anything.

### The tabs and the table

The tabs are Forex, Metals, Energy, Agriculture, Crypto and Indices. Each row is one market:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Long / Short | Bets on up and bets on down, with the weekly change underneath |
| Net | Long minus Short |
| Weekly change | How much Net moved since last week |
| Long / Short bar | The share of green (long) and red (short) |
| COT Index | The 0 to 100 position, with a white marker on the track |

You do not need the other tabs on day one. Gold is already used in your pairs. Metals and Energy can help later, for example oil with the Canadian dollar and metals with the Australian dollar.

### Look closer

At the bottom of the page, pick any market to see two charts: its net position over time for all three groups, and its COT Index over time. Look at how long the line stayed at the top or the bottom before. That shows you how long extremes can last.

### Download as CSV

The button saves the table as a spreadsheet file you can open in Excel or Google Sheets.

### The Friday Discord alert

Every Friday, shortly after the report comes out, your COT Alerts Discord server gets a message with:

- **My pairs (weekly lean):** one line for each of your pairs.
- **Markets at an extreme:** grouped by tab, with the note NEW this week when something just moved into an extreme, and the Commercials' index beside each one.
- **No longer extreme:** markets that just left an extreme.

If a message shows the same positions date as the week before, the report was probably delayed by a holiday.

## 8. A simple routine

**Friday or the weekend (10 minutes)**

1. Read the Discord message, or open the page.
2. Write down the lean for each of your pairs, plus any extremes.

**Each trading day (5 to 10 minutes)**

1. Check the economic calendar for high-impact news on your currencies.
2. Open your MT4 daily chart and pick Up, Down or Sideways on each card.
3. Ask: do the COT lean, the news and the chart agree? If not, it is fine to skip the trade.
4. If you do trade, decide your stop-loss (where you accept being wrong) and your position size **before** you click Buy or Sell.

## 9. Practice plan for the first month

Use your demo account, not real money. Keep a simple log, one row each time you look at a pair:

After four weeks, review it. How often did a strong lean match what price did? Did the lean work better when the chart agreed? That tells you how much weight to give COT in your own trading, which no seminar or guide can tell you.

## 10. Quick glossary

- **COT:** Commitments of Traders, the weekly report.
- **CFTC:** the US agency that publishes it.
- **Futures:** contracts to buy or sell at a set price on a future date.
- **Long / Short:** betting on up / betting on down.
- **Net:** long minus short.
- **COT Index:** where today's net sits in the past year's range, from 0 to 100.
- **Extreme:** an index of 10 or below, or 90 or above.
- **Hedger / Commercial:** a business using futures to protect against price swings.
- **Speculator:** a trader betting to make a profit.
- **Pip:** the smallest standard price move in a currency pair, usually the fourth decimal place (the second for yen pairs).
- **Stop-loss:** an order that closes a trade at a set loss so it cannot grow.

## 11. What this tool cannot do

- It does not predict the future. It shows how big traders were positioned on a Tuesday.
- It does not tell you when to enter or exit.
- The index depends on the lookback you choose. A lean that appears at 26 weeks but disappears at 52 weeks is a fragile one.
- It is one indicator among your other tools. Your coach's point still stands: check the news and COT before you trade, not after.
